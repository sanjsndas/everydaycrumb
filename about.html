Our story

About Everyday Crumb Corner

Simple, honest recipes & kitchen wisdom for real life.

Everyday Crumb Corner began as a personal notebook — a place to scribble down the recipes that actually worked, the ones that friends and family asked for again and again. It has since grown into a small, independent blog read by home cooks around the world. But the heart of it remains the same: approachable food, tested thoroughly, written without pretense.

Why “Everyday Crumb”?

The name is a nod to the small, everyday moments that make cooking meaningful — the crumbs on the counter after slicing a loaf, the first sip of coffee while the oven preheats, the quiet satisfaction of a meal shared. We believe that good food doesn’t have to be complicated or Instagram‑perfect. It just has to be honest.

Every recipe you’ll find here has been tested at least three times in a home kitchen. We include weights and volume measurements, substitution notes, and the little tips that make a difference. No fluff, no untested experiments — just reliable recipes you can count on.

Tested & true

Each recipe is made multiple times before it’s published. We measure carefully, note what works, and tell you when something is flexible and when it isn’t.

Everyday ingredients

You won’t find obscure flours or specialty equipment here. Our recipes use what you likely already have in your pantry — and if we suggest a swap, we explain why.

No‑nonsense writing

We skip the long personal essays before the recipe (unless there’s a truly useful story). You’ll find clear instructions, helpful notes, and a printable card at the bottom of each post.

C

Hi, I’m Clara Bennett. I spent a decade working in pastry kitchens — early mornings, precise measurements, and the relentless pursuit of the perfect croissant. These days, I cook in a small home kitchen with a slightly battered cast‑iron pan and a well‑loved sourdough starter. I started Everyday Crumb Corner because I wanted to share the kind of cooking that fits into a real life: forgiving, flavorful, and deeply satisfying.

When I’m not testing recipes, you’ll find me at the farmers’ market, reading old cookbooks, or teaching my neighbor’s kid how to crack an egg with one hand.

— Clara
